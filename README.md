## Irregular Verbs — belts and examples

Each row of `english/irregular-verbs/verbs-data.js` is `[base, past simple, past participle, Italian, {b, ex, ipa?, say?}]`: `b` is the belt (1 White … 6 Black: from the most frequent verbs to the rarest), `ex` holds two example sentences with their Italian translation (the first with the past simple, the second with the past participle; the verb form is in `[square brackets]`). Two accepted forms are written `dreamt / dreamed`.

- The level selector (List, Flashcards, Practice) works **up to a belt** (cumulative, the default) or **only one belt**; the choice is kept in the address (`?belt=2`, `?belt=4&only=1`), so a teacher can share a link to one group.
- The example sentences appear in the list, on the back of the flashcards, in the feedback and in the exercise *Irregular Verbs in context*; the PDF Worksheet Builder makes forms, translation and in-context worksheets per belt.
- New verbs get their recordings from the Audio workflow when `verbs-data.js` changes.

## Grammar sections (Modal Verbs, The Passive Voice, Word Formation)

These sections use the shared engine `assets/js/grammar-lab.js` and `assets/css/grammar-lab.css`; each section has only its colours (`app.css`) and its data:

- `data/lessons.js`: configuration and lessons (form and structure, main uses with examples and Italian translations, common mistakes, comparisons). The format is described at the top of `english/modal-verbs/data/lessons.js`.
- `data/practice-*.js`: batteries of 10–15 questions or a text with gaps (types: mc, gap, correct, transform, rewrite, text, halves, and for word formation wf and wftext). Each battery belongs to a lesson (or to `review`) and to a belt. Titles are numbered automatically per topic.

- **Word Formation** (`english/word-formation/`) follows Cambridge Use of English, Part 3. `wf` items are `[sentence with ___, WORD IN CAPITALS, [accepted answers], word class, explanation]`; `wftext` batteries are a text with `{1}…{8}` and `gaps: [[accepted answers], WORD, word class, explanation]`. List every accepted spelling (organise / organize); the word in capitals is never accepted as it is. The exam batteries (`lesson: "review"`) go from B1 (orange belt) to C2 (black belt).

New grammar sections use the full section id in the Worksheet Builder ids (older sections keep their two-letter prefix). To add a new grammar section, copy one of the folders, change `index.html`, `app.css` and the data, and add the section to `assets/js/catalog.js`.

## English Basics

`english/basics/` covers the foundations of English grammar (A1–A2, White and Yellow belts) with the shared grammar engine. Lessons add an optional `task` ("Your turn": an instruction and model sentences). Two exercise types were added to the engine for this section and can be used by every grammar section:

- `order` (Sentence building): `[words separated by " / ", [accepted sentences], explanation]`. The words are buttons that build the answer; the answer can also be typed.
- `translate` (Translation): `[Italian sentence, hint or "", [accepted English sentences], model answer, explanation]`, the same shape as in Dependent Prepositions.

- `sort` (Sorting): `{ cats: [groups], items: [[word or expression, index of the group, explanation]] }`.

Further battery options of the grammar engine: `img: [path, description]` shows a picture above the questions (such batteries are left out of the PDF Worksheet Builder); `test: true` adds the score out of 100 and the mark out of 10 (pass mark 60) to the result; `exact: true` makes a gap battery compare answers as written (used for the possessive 's, which otherwise would be read as is / has).

Contents: 16 lessons in five groups (subject pronouns, be · articles, plurals, this/that, there is · object pronouns, possessives, 's · have got, can, question words, imperative · prepositions of place and time), each with a Check yourself battery, plus mixed review batteries and a 25-question final test. Data files: `data/lessons*.js` and `data/practice-1.js` … `practice-6.js`; the picture for prepositions of place is `img/room.svg`.

The sentence checker rejects `amn't` and a contraction at the end of a clause (`Yes, I'm.`), so affirmative short answers must use the full form.

## Exercise headers

Every exercise in every section is introduced by the same three levels:

1. **TYPE** (in capitals): the kind of activity, e.g. MULTIPLE CHOICE, FILL IN THE GAPS, GAP FILL – TEXT, ERROR CORRECTION.
2. **Title** (bold): the topic, numbered when there are several exercises on it (*Present Simple – 2*, *Irregular Verbs – 3*).
   Exercises built on a text use the title of the text instead (*A new life in Dublin*).
3. **Instruction** (normal text): what the student has to do.

The type names and the default instructions are defined once, in `assets/js/exercises.js` (`window.DojoEx`), and every section uses them. Add new types there, never inside a single section.

## PDF Worksheet Builder

`worksheet/index.html` (linked as **Create a PDF Worksheet** in the header of every exercise section) builds printable A4 worksheets entirely in the browser: no server, no external service.

- **Architecture.** `assets/js/worksheet/adapters.js` has one entry per section (`SECTIONS`): the section page is loaded in a hidden frame with `?ws=1`, its data are read through the hooks the section already exposes (`__tenseDojo`, `__depprep`, `__grammar`, `__skills`, `__phon`, `__jpDojo`, `VERB_DATA`) and converted into one normalized exercise format (`{section, topic, belt, type, title, instr, parts}`). New exercises added to the data files therefore appear in the builder automatically; a new *section* only needs a new adapter entry (plus its id in `WS_SECTIONS` in `site.js` for the entry link).
- **PDF engine.** `assets/js/worksheet/pdf.js` lays out the normalized exercises (headers, page numbers, page breaks that never split a question) and writes the PDF with the vendored `pdf-lib` (`assets/js/vendor`, MIT). Fonts are embedded subsets in `assets/fonts/pdf` (Inter, Caladea, DejaVu Sans for IPA, IPA P Gothic for Japanese; licences in `licenses/`), rebuilt with `python3 tools/pdf-fonts/build.py` when new characters are added to the content. The Japanese font is downloaded only when the worksheet contains Japanese.
- **Versions.** Student worksheet (no solutions) or Teacher's version (answer key on separate pages, taken only from the solutions stored in the data, with explanations when available). Listening tasks print the questions and a reference to the audio: no transcripts are added. Writing tasks print the prompt with lined space.
- **Phonetics** practice is generated (as on the site): each PDF contains a new random selection of words and options.

## Adding content
- **News, a new resource or a status change:** edit `assets/js/catalog.js`. Each resource has an `area` (Grammar, Verbs and vocabulary, Pronunciation, Skills…, listed in `areas`) and a CEFR `level`: language pages group the resources by area, and inside an area the order of the catalog is the suggested path (Step 1, 2, 3…). Put a new resource in its area at the right point of the path. Homepage, footer and language pages update automatically.
- **A new language:** add it to `languages` in the catalog, copy `english/index.html` into a new folder (e.g. `spanish/index.html`) and change `data-lang="english"` to the new id.
- **Sounds of English (phonetics):** sound cards in `english/phonetics/data/vowels.js`, `diphthongs.js`, `consonants.js`; lessons in `lessons.js`; hand-written exercises in `exercises.js` (formats at the top of each file). Further exercises are generated automatically from the sound cards. Word audio: `audio/` + `data/audio.js`.
- **New tense exercises:** add lines to the files in `english/verb-tenses/data/` (format explained in `engine.js`). The interface groups the items of each topic into batteries of 10–15 questions automatically (by exercise type, easiest first); every long text (format `"p"`) is a battery of its own. Belt tests in the Tests tab are drawn from all topics at each level, and the texts of the *Texts and situations* topic also appear there as text tests. New data files must be added to `english/verb-tenses/index.html`.
- **Dependent Prepositions:** add expressions to `data/reference.js`; add a new test battery by copying one in `data/practice-pack.js` (formats at the top of the file).
- **Skill Development:** add tasks to `english/skills/data/` (formats in `core.js`). Reading texts and listening scripts are recorded automatically by the Audio workflow; listening scripts can use several voices.
- **Interface translations:** every new interface string in English needs its Italian version in `assets/js/i18n.js`.
- **More kanji:** add entries to `japanese/data/kanji.js` and their strokes to `strokes.js` (from KanjiVG).
- **A new learning app:** create a folder under the language (e.g. `english/phrasal-verbs/`), reuse the page shell of `english/irregular-verbs/index.html`, and set the resource `status` to `"available"` with its `url` in the catalog.

## Running and publishing
- **Publishing:** every push to `main` is published automatically to GitHub Pages
  (https://riccardosilini0-sketch.github.io/The-Language-Dojo/) by `.github/workflows/pages.yml`.
  The Netlify copy (the-language-dojo.netlify.app) is paused while the free monthly credits are used up.
- **Audio:** `.github/workflows/audio.yml` generates new recordings with Azure AI Speech when content changes.
- **Locally:** run a small web server in the folder (e.g. `python3 -m http.server`) and open http://localhost:8000.

Nothing is saved except the interface language (English or Italian), stored on the device.

## Sounds of English — sources and integrations

Main source: *English Phonetics and Phonology* (revised course slides, Prof. Riccardo Silini Nedrotti), plus the strong/weak forms exercise from the earlier PowerPoint version. The vocal-tract and vocal-fold diagrams are taken from those slides. Reference accent: SSBE; GA forms are given as broad phonetic transcriptions.

Content added to the original material (for review by the author):
- Common mistakes of Italian speakers for every sound (the slides cover only /θ ð/, h, /iː ɪ/ and /æ/).
- Minimal pairs beyond /iː–ɪ/ and /æ–e/ (e.g. /ʊ–uː/, /ɒ–ɔː/, /æ–ʌ–ɑː/, /ɜː/, /e–eɪ/, /θ–t/, /ð–d/, /s–z/, /v–w/, /ʃ–tʃ/, /dʒ–j/, /n–ŋ/, /h/–zero).
- Grapheme definition; note on /r/ as [ɹ] (phonemic vs phonetic transcription); linking r; syllabic /n/.
- Word stress: stress tendencies (two-syllable nouns/verbs, compounds, suffixes), noun–verb pairs, vowel reduction, strong and weak forms.
- Corrections: silent-letters item 2 (participated → listened: non-rhotic r is a matter of accent, not a silent letter); letter-c item 1 (exciting → decision: the /s/ of exciting is ambiguous between x and c).
- Extra examples on each sound card (all transcriptions in SSBE, Cambridge/Oxford conventions).
